# Prose tells

Adapted from pstack `unslop` (Lauren Tan, MIT). Scan the text for each pattern, then rewrite it. Keep the meaning and the intended tone.

## Content

- **-ing tails.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...". Delete them, or expand with a real source.
- **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest". Name the source or delete the claim.
- **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State the specific plan or fact.

## Words

- **AI vocabulary.** additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape, pivotal, robust, seamless, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant, elevate, unleash. Use the plain word.
- **Fancy "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Write "is" or "has".
- **Plain words.** utilize becomes use, leverage becomes use, facilitate becomes help, numerous becomes many, in the event that becomes if, in order to becomes to, due to the fact that becomes because.
- **Metaphor nouns.** substrate, wedge, vector, locus, nexus, primitive, surface, bedrock, scaffolding, paradigm, north star, flywheel, endgame. Use the concrete word: base, add, way, the last phase.
- **Synonym cycling.** One name per concept, repeated.
- **Adverbs on weak verbs.** "runs quickly" becomes the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured change.

## Structure

- **"Not just X, but Y"** and "It's not X, it's Y". State the point directly.
- **Rule of three.** Use the natural number of items.
- **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on one scale. List the topics.
- **Dense sentences.** If a reader must backtrack, split the sentence. One idea per sentence.
- **Passive voice.** Name the actor: "the loader parses the file". Keep the passive only when the actor is unknown or does not matter.
- **Mannered prose.** Aphorisms, personified code, figurative verbs ("rides along"). Say it literally.
- **Over-compression.** Dropped articles, verbless fragments, arrows, and private abbreviations. Write whole sentences.
- **Say what it does.** If a sentence could appear unchanged in another project's docs, it says nothing about this one. Cut it, or replace it with an instruction, a fact, or a number.

## Punctuation and format

- **Em dashes.** None. Use a period or a comma.
- **Mid-sentence colons.** A colon is fine before a list or an example, not as a hinge between two clauses.
- **Bold.** Only for a lead-in that names an item and ends in a period. Not for every proper noun.
- **Inline-header lists** ("**Performance:** Performance improved..."). Convert them to sentences.
- **Title Case Headings.** Use sentence case.
- **Emoji** in headings and bullets. Remove them.
- **Curly quotes.** Use straight quotes.

## Chat artifacts

- "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Certainly!", "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!". Delete them and answer directly.
- Hedging stacks ("could potentially possibly"). Use one word: "may".
- Signposting ("Let's dive in", "Here's the thing"). Delete it.
- Tidy closers ("the rest follows", "it all falls out"). Delete them.
